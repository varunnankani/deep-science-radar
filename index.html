How the radar works

A scheduled run checks public signals every morning, then adds or updates companies here.

Who is included

  • Core scouting universe: Indian companies building hard technology (space, chips, energy, biology, robotics, quantum, materials, medtech) whose last confirmed round is Series A or earlier, or whose stage is undisclosed.
  • Benchmarks: later-stage or listed companies (for example Ather, ideaForge, Skyroot, Pixxel) kept for comparison. They are labelled and left out of the default lists.
  • Companies come from funding news, government grants (IN-SPACe, BIRAC, National Quantum Mission) and incubator announcements. This means coverage leans towards companies that already have investors.

How assessments work

  • Each factor is scored 1 to 5 only when there is public evidence for it, and the evidence is shown next to the score.
  • A factor without evidence shows Insufficient evidence. It is never filled with an average.
  • The radar score is the weighted average of the factors that have evidence. It needs at least three of the four; with three it is marked partial, and with fewer there is no overall score.
  • Evidence confidence rates the sources, separately from the assessment.

Stage and signals

  • Last confirmed round is the most recent round reported as closed, with its stage as the coverage states it. If no stage is stated it reads Undisclosed.
  • Latest signal is the newest event of any kind: funding, a grant, a technical milestone or reported discussions. Reported discussions never change the confirmed stage.
  • Event date is when it happened; source date is when it was reported.

TRL and the RDI scheme

  • The ₹1 lakh crore Research, Development and Innovation fund lends long-term at low or nil interest to R&D at TRL 4 and above, through fund managers such as TDB and BIRAC.
  • Each TRL estimate cites the milestone it rests on. Without a demonstrated milestone the TRL reads Not estimable.
  • The radar never confirms eligibility. Labels read "eligibility unverified" until a fund manager says otherwise.

Dates on every profile

  • Assessed on: when the scores were last set.
  • Last substantive update: when facts on the profile last changed.
  • Last verified: when the sources were last re-checked, even if nothing changed.
  • Date added: when the company entered the dataset. In the network this is kept apart from the date of a relationship.

Limits

  • The radar does not judge whether the science works. It reports what has been demonstrated publicly.
  • International peers and technology groupings are the radar's own comparisons and are drawn as dashed lines.
  • LinkedIn links for people open a LinkedIn search, not a confirmed profile.
